Cue-Preserving Binaural Speech Enhancement with Dual-Path and Polar-Decoupling Network

Aleksej Chinaev, Gerald Enzner

Abstract

Binaural speech enhancement faces a fundamental trade-off between achieving effective noise reduction and preserving the spatial-cues of the acoustic scene. Since a constraining of binaural denoising to the preservation of binaural cues for speech and noise contributes to a natural and spatially faithful sound perception, even in challenging acoustic environments, this paper proposes a novel common-gain and -phase (CoGaPha) network for lightweight, causal, STFT-based, phase-sensitive and cue-preserving denoising. The CoGaPha network combines fresh ingredients of the modern speech technology, such as dual-path time-frequency processing using recurrent Mamba architectures and magnitude-phase (polar) decoupling with separate CoGaPha decoders for spectral gain and phase. An experimental evaluation on various binaural datasets with a rich variety of noise types reveals the benefits of the proposed denoising in terms of intelligibility, signal quality and noise reduction. Further, a framework for evaluating the ability of considered networks to generalize for noise types unseen in training is reported. Compared to both internal ablations and external baselines, the proposed CoGaPha network demonstrates excellent denoising capabilities and small generalization gap, including the hearing-aid application.

Audio samples

In the following, we provide binaural output signals of 15 neural networks that are involved in evaluation of the proposed CoGaPha system. The audio samples are available for two datasets "testKiekko" and "testWham" (the networks beeing optimized on "trainKiekko" data) generated for various conditions of ambient noise including a bathroom shower, a church visit, a restaurant, and an office building and many more noise types. The materials demonstrate denosing performance of the considered systems in order to support personal listening experience. The audio samples are denoised outputs of twelve DNNs for CoGaPha-related ablation study and three external baselines such as BinMap(FCRN), BinConvTasNet and CoGa(CDAE). Every audio sample was generated for individual noise type, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and direction of arrival (DOA).

Ablation models are configured with:
  • two system setups, i.e, bilateral (Bil) vs. binaural (Bin),
  • three regression styles, i.e., mapping (Map) and real-valued spectral gain (Ga) regression vs. the gain-phase (GaPha) regression in our proposed CoGaPha system,
  • two ''inner'' architectures,i.e, complex convolutional recurrent (cCRN) vs. the proposed dual-path time-frequency cCRN (DPcCRN) model.
